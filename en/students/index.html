Standards check

Remarks you won’t hear again

The standards checker’s red pen keeps catching the same things. Here is what Opir takes off your hands.

REMARK 1

“The parts list doesn’t match the schematic”

A table in Word lives a life of its own: you change R7 on the schematic and forget it in the list. In Opir the parts list is built from the schematic and always matches it. Groups by letter code, identical parts in a row on one line (VT1, VT2), blank lines between groups, form 2 on the first sheet and 2a on the next. The document code switches to the parts list’s by itself.

REMARK 2

“The title block is filled in wrong”

Designation, product name, names, letter, group: once in the document’s properties, and the same on every sheet. The document name, the sheet numbers and their count fill themselves, separately for the schematic and the parts list. The lettering is in a drafting font, in Ukrainian, Russian or English: the language is chosen per document.

REMARK 3

“Reference designators out of order”

Add a part in the middle and the numbering falls apart. Renumber Components… puts the numbers back in order: by columns top to bottom or by rows left to right. And Check Schematic finds before you hand it in what your tutor would find: duplicate designators, unnumbered R?, unconnected pins, parts without a value.

REMARK 4

“Explain how the circuit works”

The main question at the defence. Ask the assistant: it sees the same schematic you do. It explains what each part is for, works out the stage’s DC operating point and suggests what to tell the panel. Where it takes parameters from memory because no datasheet is attached, it says so.

Small things that eat an evening

A circuit from the manual

Photograph or scan the circuit and put it under the sheet as an underlay. Place the parts over it, or ask the assistant to place them from the photo.

Printing and the written report

Vector PDF on A4 or A3, ready for a print shop. PNG at 300 dpi to put the schematic into your report without blur.

Your school’s own frame

Your guidelines want a different frame or title block? Draw the form once in the form editor and use it in every document.

ESKD symbols

Over 140 graphic symbols, letter codes, line widths from the GOST 2.303 series. Missing one? Make it from a similar symbol with New Symbol from This…

The datasheet with the part

Drop a PDF on a chip and it is kept in the document. The assistant takes the pinout from it, not from memory.

A native Mac app

No Windows, no virtual machines, no cracked copies. The schematic is an ordinary file: move it to another Mac and carry on.

Questions

What people usually ask

Isn’t that cheating?

You draw it, and you answer at the defence. The assistant is like a tutor: it explains, calculates and points out mistakes. It doesn’t lay wires, and it marks its own estimates as such.

What if I’m on Windows?

For now Opir is for macOS only, version 15 or later.

Do I need the assistant to do my coursework?

No. Drawing, numbering, checking and printing work without it and without the internet. The assistant runs on a model you choose: a cloud one with your key, or a local one in LM Studio or Ollama.

The library lacks the part I need

Make one: New Symbol from This… in the context menu of a similar symbol. Pin names and numbers are set in the symbol editor.
