What it is

A drawing board that understands the circuit

Opir knows what is connected to what: connections are nets, not just lines. That is why it keeps the parts list itself, checks the schematic and can talk about it with the assistant.

Drawings to standard

The sheet looks the way it will print: black on white, a frame, a title block.

  • Over 140 symbols, GOST or US
  • The parts list compiles itself
  • Your own components and sheet forms
  • A part keeps its datasheet with it

Assistant

Describe the circuit in words — it picks the parts, connects them and lays them out on the sheet.

  • Builds a circuit from a description
  • Digitises a photo of an old schematic
  • Checks and explains what it finds
  • Goes by the parts’ datasheets

Switchboards

Breakers, RCDs, relays, N and PE bars — and everything the installer needs, on one sheet.

  • The table of groups works out current and voltage drop
  • Wire sections and colours
  • The “breaker protects the wire” check
Assistant and MCP

Your model, your rules

The assistant works through the model you choose and sees the same schematic you do: parts, nets, check results.

  • A cloud or a local modelAnthropic, any OpenAI-compatible service, or a local model in LM Studio or Ollama. With a local one the schematic never leaves your computer.
  • A datasheet with its partDrop a PDF on a part: it is kept inside the document, always at hand. The assistant takes the pinout and the ratings from it, not from memory.
  • Every edit is one undo stepWhat the assistant changed glows green on the sheet. Don’t like it — ⌘Z.
  • Honest about its limitsThe assistant says apart what the program calculates and what is the model’s own judgement.
  • MCP for Claude Desktop and Claude CodeAn outside client works with the open document through a local server, on 127.0.0.1 only. The Copy Client Configuration button in Settings gives the ready configuration — or write it by hand:
    { "mcpServers": { "opir": { "command": "/Applications/Opir.app/Contents/Helpers/opir", "args": ["mcp"] } } }
Standards

What follows a standard, and what is for reference

A program for hobbyists and enthusiasts does not replace a design engineer. So we say plainly what you can rely on.

By the text of the standard

  • Symbols and letter codes — ESKD (the GOST 2 series)
  • Frame, title block, parts list
  • Wire colour codes

For reference, with the source

  • Permissible cable current — PUE:2026, Ukraine’s installation code, with the table number
  • Voltage drop along a line
  • Total load and supply current

Not checked

  • Short-circuit currents
  • Selectivity of protection
  • Grouped cables and temperature

Questions

The main things, briefly

Do I need the assistant to use the program?

No. It is an ordinary schematic editor: a library, wires, a check, printing. The assistant is a helper, and you need never turn it on.

Where does my schematic go when I ask the assistant?

To the model you chose in Settings. With a cloud service — to its servers; with a local model the schematic stays on your computer.

Why doesn’t the assistant draw the wires itself?

It joins pins with nets — they show as dashed lines. A person lays the wires: the schematic is easier to read and check that way. A single net can be routed by a command.

Can I take a schematic to KiCad?

Yes, as a netlist. There is also export for SPICE and JSON, and a bill of materials in CSV.

And circuit boards?

No. Opir is about schematic diagrams and switchboards; board layout stays with professional CAD.
