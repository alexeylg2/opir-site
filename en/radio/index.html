On the workbench

Four questions before you switch on the HT

And how Opir answers them.

QUESTION 1

“The circuit in the old magazine is barely legible”

Photograph the page and put it under the sheet. The assistant reads the parts and nets and places them as on the original; you lay the wires yourself against the picture. Values it can’t make out on the photo, it marks as doubtful.

QUESTION 2

“Is the valve overloaded?”

The assistant works out the 6P3S’s operating point from the drawing: cathode bias on R6, anode current, anode dissipation at about 85–90 % of the limit. And it spots what is easy to miss: only the screen current flows through R7, so the “+250 V” rail sits at about 290 V. It takes the valve data from memory and says so.

RequestCheck the amplifier: what operating point does the 6P3S run at, is the anode dissipation within limits, and are the heaters wired right? Don’t change anything.
QUESTION 3

“Heaters and supply without a cobweb”

All the heaters go by the labels H1 and H2 from the heater winding, the HT by the signs “+300 V” and “+250 V”. The drawing stays readable, and the program knows what connects to what: one name, one net. The double triode’s heater section can sit apart, by the power supply.

QUESTION 4

“What do I buy and what do I wind?”

The parts list builds itself from the drawing: valves, transformers, 450 V capacitors, resistors, each with its value. A double triode is one line however many of its sections are on the sheet. The bill of materials saves as CSV.

In the library

For valves and receivers

Valves to GOST

Triode, double triode, beam tetrode, pentode, rectifier valve, with heater pins. A double triode’s sections are placed separately.

Transformers and chokes

Centre-tapped, with two secondaries, plain; coils with a tap and with a tuning core.

For a receiver

Variable and trimmer capacitors, a crystal, whip and ferrite antennas, a rotary switch.

Labels and supply signs

One name, one net: heaters, HT, a signal from sheet to sheet.

The valve’s datasheet

Attach the valve’s data sheet and the assistant takes the limits from it, not from memory.

SPICE and KiCad

A netlist for SPICE and KiCad. Opir ships no valve models; you bring your own.

Honestly

What stays with you

The program does not calculate valve operating points: the assistant estimates currents and dissipation from the datasheet or from memory and says which. Check by measuring.

The HT is dangerous even after switch-off: discharge the filter capacitors before reaching into the wiring.

Questions

What people usually ask

The valve I need isn’t there — what now?

Take the nearest by construction, a triode, pentode or tetrode, and write its type in the value. If the construction differs, New Symbol from This… on a similar valve.

And solid-state builds?

Just the same: transistors, ICs, regulators, optocouplers are in the library, and the assistant and the check work alike.
