At the bench

Four situations from any repair bench

And how they look in Opir.

CASE 1

“There’s no schematic for this unit”

Photograph the board and put the photo under the sheet as an underlay. The assistant reads the markings, “202” on R3, “101” on R1, and puts the symbols where the parts are. It renames BD1, D1, ZD1 to VD1–VD3 as GOST wants, keeps the factory labels in the notes, and marks what it can’t make out on the photo as doubtful. Then you join them up against the board.

CASE 2

“It won’t start. Where do I begin?”

Describe what you have measured. The assistant sees the schematic and the parts list, so it talks about your unit, not switch-mode supplies in general: VCC on pin 7 of DA1, then the start-up resistor R1, C5, VD3, the oscillator on pin 4. And it starts with safety: measure against the minus of C1, through an isolation transformer.

RequestThe adapter won’t start: 0 V at the output, the fuse is fine, C1 has 310 V. What do I check, and in what order?
CASE 3

“What’s the pinout here?”

Drop the datasheet on the chip and the PDF is kept in the document. The assistant takes the pinout and the limits from it, with the page number, not from memory; where there is no datasheet, it says so. A chip can get a symbol of its own with pins by function, like the UC3843 here; the numbers are the package’s.

CASE 4

“What’s already been replaced in this unit?”

Write the replacements into the notes and the parts list becomes a repair record: R1 open, C5 dried out. The assistant takes that history into account: R1 is its first suspect, because a 0.25 W resistor overheats there. A bill of materials in CSV to order parts, a PDF for the customer.

On the bench

What else comes in handy

The whole net

Click a net in the panel and all its pins light up. You see where a trace goes, even where it hides under a part on the board.

Hot and cold ground

The chassis sign for the primary, ⏚ for the output: the drawing shows at once where the isolation lies.

Check Schematic

Unconnected pins, a net with one pin, two outputs on one net: mistakes in the redrawing show before you pick up the iron.

A symbol from a similar one

A part missing from the library? New Symbol from This… on a similar one, with pins and numbers as on the package.

Datasheets stay put

The PDF of a part you removed or replaced stays in the document, in a folder of unused ones.

Export

PDF for the customer, a CSV bill of materials for ordering, a netlist for KiCad and SPICE.

Honestly

What stays with you

The assistant builds the order of checks from the schematic and what you have told it; without datasheets, from memory, and it says so. Measuring, deciding and answering for the repair are yours.

The primary side of a switch-mode supply sits at mains voltage. An isolation transformer, a discharged capacitor, one hand in your pocket.

Questions

What people usually ask

Does Opir know SMD marking codes?

No, there is no code book. The assistant may suggest one from memory, but that is its estimate; check it against the datasheet.

Can I take a schematic from a service manual?

Yes: put the scanned page under the sheet and the assistant places the parts from the picture. You lay the wires against the original, which makes a misreading easy to spot.

And PCB layout?

No. Opir is about schematics; boards stay with professional PCB tools.
