On site

Four jobs they call the electrician for

And how Opir makes them easier.

JOB 1

“There’s a cabinet, but no drawing”

The previous electrician left a cabinet and not a single sheet. Redraw it: power on the left, control on the right. KM1’s coil sits in the control circuit, its main contacts in the power circuit, its auxiliary contacts where they work, and the program knows it is one starter: one line in the parts list.

JOB 2

“A reverser without an interlock is a phase-to-phase short”

KM2 swaps L1 and L3; let it close together with KM1 and you have a phase-to-phase short. Ask the assistant: it follows the nets, confirms the interlock through the break contacts KM1 21-22 and KM2 21-22, reminds you of push-button and mechanical interlocks, and says which of that is its own estimate.

RequestCheck the reversing starter: can KM1 and KM2 come on together, what happens when KK1 trips, and how do I add a pause before the direction changes? Don’t change anything.
JOB 3

“And what happens when the overload trips?”

Contact 95-96 of KK1 opens the control circuit ahead of Stop: on an overload the coil drops out, the hold-in is lost and the motor stays off until started again. The drawing shows it at a glance, and also where a time relay goes for a pause before reversing.

JOB 4

“What do I buy for the cabinet?”

The parts list comes from the drawing: breakers, contactors, the overload relay, push-buttons, lamps, each device once however its sections are spread. A bill of materials in CSV, the drawing for the cabinet door in PDF.

In the library

All a control drawing needs

Contactors and relays

Two-, three- and four-pole contactors with coil and auxiliary contacts, control relays, impulse relays.

Time relays

On-delay and off-delay, for pauses before reversing and delayed starts.

Overload relay

Heaters in the power circuit, contacts 95-96 and 97-98 in the control circuit.

Three-phase motor

To GOST 2.722: a circle with M and three leads, U1, V1, W1.

Buttons and lamps

Push-buttons with make and break contacts, signal lamps.

Wire colours

L1 brown, L2 black, L3 grey, N blue, as in the cabinet.

Honestly

What isn’t there yet

There are no cross-references by the contacts yet, such as “coil on sheet 2, zone C3”: Opir keeps a device’s sections together but labels them on the sheet only by designation. The library has no PLC or variable-frequency drive symbols either; a module can be drawn as a symbol of its own.

The assistant goes through interlock logic like a consultant and marks its own estimates as such. The cabinet’s safety is the electrician’s responsibility.

Questions

What people usually ask

And star-delta?

It is built from the same parts: three contactors, a time relay, an overload relay. Ask the assistant: it places the devices and joins them into nets, and you lay the wires.

Can power and control go on separate sheets?

Yes. A device’s sections can sit on different sheets, and it is still one line in the parts list.
